What is a DoDAAC?

A DoDAACDepartment of Defense Activity Address Code — a 6-character alphanumeric identifier (Department of Defense Activity Address Code) is a 6-character alphanumeric code assigned by the DLADefense Logistics Agency — manages DoDAAC assignments and the DODAAD database (Defense Logistics Agency) to uniquely identify a DoD activity, unit, or organization for the purpose of ordering, shipping, billing, and accountability of supplies and services.

Key Purposes

  • Identify the ordering activity (requisitioner)
  • Identify the funding/billing activity (bill-to)
  • Identify the ship-to / delivery location
  • Track accountability for government property
  • Enable interfacing with WAWFWide Area Workflow — DoD electronic invoicing system (now a module inside PIEE), PIEEProcurement Integrated Enterprise Environment — DoD acquisition portal, DAASDefense Automatic Addressing System — routes DoD logistics messages, and other DoD systems

Important: DoDAACs are NOT the same as UICUnit Identification Code — Army/Marine unit identifier (different from DoDAAC) (Unit Identification Codes), CAGE CodesCommercial and Government Entity code — identifies contractors, NOT DoD activities, or contract numbers — though they often appear together in procurement documents.

Why It Matters

DoDAACs are foundational to the entire defense acquisition lifecycle. Without a valid, active DoDAAC, a unit cannot requisition supplies, receive shipments, process invoices in WAWF, or maintain accountability of government property. They serve as the universal address in virtually every DoD logistics and procurement system.

DoDAAC Types

All 15 functional DoDAAC types per AFMAN 23-230, plus Signal/Authority Code interaction guidance.

1. Requisitioning DoDAAC (RQ)

Used to place orders and requisitions for supplies and services. The activity identified by this DoDAAC is responsible for the requirement. Must be registered in PIEEProcurement Integrated Enterprise Environment — DoD acquisition portal and authorized by the DoD Component. The RQ DoDAAC appears on every requisition document (MILSTRIP, purchase request, etc.) and links the order to the requesting unit.

2. Ship-To DoDAAC (ST)

Identifies the physical location where supplies are to be delivered. Must include a valid DODAADDepartment of Defense Activity Address Directory — master DoDAAC database address entry with complete street address, city, state, and ZIP code. Critical for transportation routing and carrier delivery. An incorrect or missing Ship-To address is one of the most common causes of delivery delays.

3. Bill-To DoDAAC (BT)

Identifies the activity responsible for payment and funding. Links to a LOALine of Accounting — funding citation for government purchases (Line of Accounting) and appropriation data. Must be active and funded in the relevant fiscal year (FYFiscal Year — DoD fiscal year runs Oct 1 – Sep 30). Finance offices use this DoDAAC to route invoices and obligate funds.

4. Supplementary Address / Mark-For DoDAAC (MF)

A secondary routing identifier (formally "Supplementary Address" per DLM 4000.25, rp 45-50) placed on shipping containers to direct delivery to a sub-activity within a larger installation. The Mark-For DoDAAC is printed on the exterior packaging to help the receiving warehouse or mailroom route the shipment to the correct tenant unit. Not always required — typically used on large installations with multiple tenant organizations.

5. Admin DoDAAC

Used for contract administration — identifies the Contract Administration Office (CAO) or Contracting Officer. Commonly associated with DCMADefense Contract Management Agency — administers contracts post-award or local contracting offices. The Admin DoDAAC ensures proper oversight and administration of DoD contracts after award.

6. Signal Code Combinations

Signal CodesSingle-letter code defining ship-to and bill-to DoDAAC relationships per DLM 4000.25 Appendix 7.10 (single letters A–Z) combine DoDAAC types to determine who pays and where to ship. They are a critical part of every requisition.

7. Contractor DoDAAC (Air Force E-Prefix)

Per AFMAN 23-230 Table 2.2, Air Force contractor DoDAACs use the 'E' prefix and are further categorized by sub-type:

  • EBICP (Inventory Control Point): Inventory location for customer shipments of Contractor ICP-managed material. NOT used for requisitioning.
  • EDDepot: Inventory location for Contractor ICP. NOT used for requisitioning.
  • EJAircraft Engines: Assigned to contractors performing new production and repair contracts for aircraft engines.
  • EPFuels: Assigned to contractors requiring aviation fuels when producing, repairing, or testing Air Force aircraft.
  • EYProduction, Manufacturing, R&D, Test, ICS, CLS, TCT, O&M Contracts: Broadest contractor category; covers production/manufacturing, R&D, test, Interim Contractor Support (ICS), Contractor Logistics Support (CLS, EEIC 578), Total Contract Training (TCT), and Operations & Maintenance contracts where GFE/GFP is involved.

Contractor DoDAACs are managed by AFMC/AFSC and are tied to specific contract numbers. They cannot be used for general requisitioning. Authority Code is typically 07 (Administrative) or 05 (Non-Requisition). They are distinct from CAGE codes — a contractor may hold both a CAGE code (for contract identification) and a Contractor DoDAAC (for logistics/supply chain actions).

8. Training DoDAAC

Used by schools, training detachments, and Professional Military Education (PME) institutions. Supports requisitioning of training aids, equipment, and supplies unique to educational missions. Interfaces with AETC systems and LIMS-EV.

9. Transportation DoDAAC

Used by movement control units, freight offices, and transportation management offices (TMOs). Routes shipping transactions, freight bills, and GTC transactions through DAAS. Interfaces with GTN (Global Transportation Network) and DPS (Defense Personal Property System).

10. Depot / Maintenance DoDAAC

Used by Air Logistics Complexes (ALCs), depots, and organic repair/overhaul facilities. Interfaces with AFMC, LMP, and D200 logistics systems for work orders and parts accountability. These DoDAACs carry high transaction volumes and are critical for depot-level maintenance scheduling and parts requisitioning.

11. Medical DoDAAC

Used by Military Treatment Facilities (MTFs) and medical logistics activities. Interfaces with DMLSS (Defense Medical Logistics Standard Support) and DHA supply chains. Medical DoDAACs are managed under the Defense Health Agency and may have specialized supply routing for pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and blood products.

12. Research & Development DoDAAC

Assigned to AFRL, test centers (e.g., AFTC, AFOTEC), and developmental labs. Used for ordering specialized R&D materials, test assets, and prototype components. These DoDAACs may interface with DTIC (Defense Technical Information Center) and specialized test center ERP systems.

13. National Guard / Reserve DoDAAC

Used by Air National Guard (ANG) and Air Force Reserve Command (AFRC) units. May have different funding lines (O&M vs. Title 32 funds) affecting signal code routing. When a Reserve/ANG unit is federalized (Title 10), funding and signal code logic may change — coordinate with both the state and federal resource management offices.

14. Non-Appropriated Fund (NAF) DoDAAC

Used for AAFES, lodging, MWR, clubs, and other NAF activities. Funded outside appropriated funds — cannot be used for APF (Appropriated Fund) purchases and cannot be mixed with APF funding lines. NAF DoDAACs use separate accounting systems and their own funding streams. Ensure the signal code does not route billing to an APF Bill-To DoDAAC.

15. Special Program DoDAAC

Used for Special Access Programs (SAP), SCI, nuclear logistics, and other restricted programs. Access to these DoDAACs in DoDAAD may be restricted or require special clearance/permissions. These codes may not appear in standard DoDAAD queries — contact your Special Programs office or Security Manager for lookup assistance.

Signal Code + Authority Code Interaction

When a DoDAAC has a non-standard Authority Code, signal codes must be selected carefully to avoid funding mismatches and transaction rejections:

  • Authority Code 05 (Non-Requisition / Contractor) — Cannot initiate requisitions. Use only as Ship-To or Bill-To when designated by another activity's signal code.
  • Authority Code 07 (Administrative) — Cannot requisition, ship-to, or bill-to. Information/identification only. Standard DAAS routing does not apply.
  • Authority Code 06 (Free Issue) — Restricted to no-cost items. May only be used with Signal Code D or M. Never route billing through an APF Bill-To DoDAAC.
  • Authority Code 03 (Do Not Ship-To) — Can requisition and bill, but cannot be a ship-to destination. Must use Signal Code J, K, L, M, or X when requisitioning.

How to Request a DoDAAC

1

Determine Eligibility

Verify the requesting activity is a legitimate DoD organization. Contractors CANNOT hold standard DoDAACs — they use CAGE codes (though Contractor DoDAACs with specific Authority Codes exist for specific logistics functions). The activity must have a documented mission requirement for ordering, receiving, or funding government supplies and services.

Submit via the Correct Channel

Army

Submit through G4/Logistics Officer to Army DoDAAC Manager at HRC or LOGSA.

Navy

Submit via N4/N6 to NAVSUP BSC DoDAAC Program Office.

Air Force

Submit through A4 to AFMC/AFSC DoDAAC Manager.

DLA

Submit via DLA Enterprise DoDAAC Management (DEDM) system.

Other Agencies

Contact DLA Transaction Services — the central DoDAAC registry authority for all other DoD components.

4

Processing & Approval

Standard processing: 3–5 business days. Expedited requests are available with commander justification. DLA Transaction Services assigns the 6-character code and loads it into the DODAAD database. You'll receive notification upon assignment.

5

Activation & Verification

After assignment: verify your DoDAAC in PIEE/WAWF within 24–48 hours. Request system access for authorized users. Coordinate with your Finance/Resource Management office to link to funding lines (LOA).

6

Annual Validation

DoDAACs must be validated annually (typically in Q4 of the fiscal year, July–September). Inactive DoDAACs may be suspended or deactivated. Suspensions require a reactivation memo signed by the Commander/Director.

Common Lookup Errors
  • "Invalid DoDAAC" — Code not in DODAAD; may be deactivated or a typo. Verify all 6 characters.
  • "Suspended DoDAAC" — Reactivation required; contact your Component DoDAAC Manager.
  • "Address Not Found" — TAC 2 (Ship-to) address missing from DODAAD; submit an update request.
  • "Authority Code Mismatch" — DoDAAC exists but Authority Code doesn't support the requested function; contact your DoDAAC Manager to verify or update.

Troubleshooting

My DoDAAC shows as "Suspended" in WAWF

Contact your Component DoDAAC Manager with a Reactivation Request memo signed by the Commander/Director. Include justification and updated POC info. Processing takes 3–5 business days. Ensure your annual validation was completed — suspensions are most commonly caused by missed annual reviews.

I received a shipment with the wrong DoDAAC

Do not refuse the shipment. Document the discrepancy on the receiving report. Contact your supply NCO/officer and the contracting officer. Submit a corrected receiving report in WAWF if applicable. The discrepancy may stem from a Signal Code error on the original requisition.

My DoDAAC is not recognized in PIEE/WAWF

New DoDAACs can take 24–72 hours to propagate to all systems after assignment. If the issue persists after 72 hours, contact the DLA Customer Support with your DoDAAC assignment confirmation.

I need to update my ship-to address

Submit a DoDAAC Update Request through your Component DoDAAC Manager. Include the new TAC 2 (freight) address, effective date, and commander signature. Updates reflect in DODAAD within 2–3 business days. Ensure all downstream systems (GCSS-Army, LMP, etc.) also update.

Multiple activities share a DoDAAC — is this allowed?

Generally no. Each distinct activity requiring separate ordering, billing, or shipping should have its own DoDAAC. Shared DoDAACs can cause accountability and audit issues, complicate property tracking, and create invoice routing problems. Request separate codes through your DoDAAC Manager.

My DoDAAC was deactivated during the annual review

If the activity is still operational, submit a Reactivation Request immediately with updated information. If the activity was deactivated in error, escalate to your Component Program Manager. Provide documentation of ongoing missions and current operational status.

Contractor keeps asking for my DoDAAC — should I share it?

Yes — DoDAACs on active contracts are not classified. Contractors need your Ship-To and Bill-To DoDAACs to correctly fill out invoices in WAWF and shipping documents. Never provide your DoDAAC for activities outside the scope of the contract.

How do I request a DoDAAC for a deployed/contingency unit?

Contingency DoDAACs follow the same process but may be expedited. Contact your Theater Sustainment Command J4 or equivalent. Temporary/exercise DoDAACs can also be issued for training events. Ensure the deployed unit's APO/FPO address is loaded into DODAAD.
